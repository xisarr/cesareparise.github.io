Cesare V. Parise

Senior Lecturer, Department of Psychology, University of Liverpool

I am a perception scientist with a dual nature. As a blue-sky researcher, I investigate how the senses interact to generate a coherent representation of our body and environment, through the development of stimulus-computable theoretical models.

Beyond fundamental research, a quantitative understanding of multisensory perception and action paves the way for next-generation interfaces for immersive technologies and sensory augmentation.

Perception Scientist Applied Perception Scientist Multisensory Integration Haptics & Spatial Hearing

Industry & Collaboration

As an applied perception scientist, I work with companies developing multimodal interfaces and immersive products. I can help you:

  • Characterize the response of multimodal interfaces, measuring how users perceive and react to visual, auditory, and haptic output.
  • Analyse these response profiles against human perceptual skills, identifying where a design matches or exceeds perceptual capacity.
  • Optimize interface design and control, so that cues are perceived as intended with less effort and fewer errors.

For consulting, applied research, perceptual evaluation, or licensing enquiries, please get in touch.

Research

My work asks how the senses combine information, and how that combination shapes perception and action.

Research themes

Audiovisual correlation and causal inference

How the brain detects when signals from different senses belong together.

Multisensory integration

Optimal cue combination and the illusions that reveal its rules.

Haptics

Touch and force feedback as channels for interaction and perception.

Spatial hearing

How listeners localise sounds and integrate them with vision and touch.

Key paper

Correlation detection as a stimulus computable account for audiovisual perception, causal inference, and saliency maps in mammals

eLife (2026) · Parise & Ernst

An image- and sound-computable population model of audiovisual perception. Optimal cue integration, Bayesian causal inference, spatial orienting, and several multisensory illusions emerge from a population of multisensory correlation detectors.
